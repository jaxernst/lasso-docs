---
title: "Consuming pages correctly"
description: "Apply pages atomically, handle reorgs and errors, and resume SSE from the cursor your database committed."
---

A cursor belongs to your consumer's committed state. Apply pages serially and
resume from the last saved `next`, whether you use SSE or polling.
The [integration guides](/cloud/streams/integration) implement this contract.

```text
BEGIN
  delete this source's rows where block_number >= page.from
  insert page.items
  save page.next
COMMIT
```

Scope all three operations to the same local source identity: one fixed chain,
source and filter. Put derived model updates in the same transaction. Update the
in-memory position only after commit. A database failure must stop the consumer
and close its connection; fix it before restarting.

## Reorgs and empty pages

A correction page starts lower and carries `reorg: true`. The same delete-and-insert
rule replaces orphaned rows without special reorg logic. Corrections can rewind
below your original start block. Do not clamp `page.from` to that start.

A page with no items and unchanged `next` means nothing new yet; applying it is
a no-op. Other empty pages can advance through blocks with no matching events.
Do not skip their cursors.

`through`, `safe` and `finalized` are block number/hash pairs and may be null.
`tip` is a decimal block number or null. Page numbers are decimal; item quantities
are JSON-RPC hex. Keep the highest finalized mark across lower or null marks and
process restarts when gating [outbox effects](/cloud/streams/integration#side-effects-at-the-tip).

## Resume from your own cursor

SSE frames use `id: <page.next>` and `data: <JSON page>`, with no `event` field.
Reconnect with `Last-Event-ID: <saved next>` or `from=<saved next>`. The header
wins and accepts a block number or cursor, never `latest`.

EventSource can remember a received event ID before your database commits that
page. Its automatic reconnect can skip uncommitted work. Close it on disconnect,
finish queued transactions, then reconnect yourself from the committed cursor.
The dashboard's example follows this pattern. The fetch consumer in the guide
instead reads and commits one page at a time.

Apply only complete pages. On a truncated body, incomplete SSE frame or network
failure, discard the partial page and retry the saved position. SSE writes a
page at least every 30 seconds; reconnect after 60 seconds of silence.

## Handle refusals

| Status | Code | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 400 | `invalid_parameter` | Fix the request. Pass `next` as `from`, not `cursor`. Start fresh after changing filters. |
| 401 | `unauthorized` | Stop; use an enabled current key. |
| 402 | `balance_exhausted` | Stop; fund within your budget, then resume the saved position. |
| 403 | `not_entitled` | Stop; use an account with beta access. |
| 404 | `chain_not_served` | Choose a supported chain. |
| 409 | `resync_required` | Clear this source's rows and checkpoint together, then rebuild from your own start block. |
| 429 | `too_many_readers`, `rate_limit_exceeded` | Close extra readers and back off; honor `Retry-After` when present. |
| 503 | `chain_unavailable` | Retry the same position after `Retry-After`, always 1 to 30 seconds. |

A 409 is not a transient failure. For a durable rebuild, keep a numeric start
block; restarting from `latest` cannot reconstruct earlier materialized history.
Refusals do not advance your position.

Before the first SSE page, failures return HTTP status and JSON. After SSE has
started, failures arrive as `event: error` controls with no ID, then the connection
closes. Controls are not pages. Retry `chain_unavailable` from your saved position,
using `retry_after`; rebuild on `resync_required` and stop on auth or funding errors.

Errors normally use `{"error":"code"}` with optional details. A 402 instead nests
`code`, `message`, `challenges`, `credit` and `next_action` inside `error`.
Its SSE control includes `retry: 60000`. Inspect the returned funding action
privately and [top up](/cloud/balance-and-fallback#top-up) within your budget.

## Limits

Keep `next` opaque. It is valid only for the same chain, source and filter.
Pages span at most 10,000 whole blocks, targeting 1 MiB of items with a 4 MiB
ceiling. An oversized block is delivered whole, alone, or returns 503.
