# The unified interface for blockchain RPC

Lasso wrangles your nodes and providers into one high-performance, edge-routed
JSON-RPC endpoint. Its routing engine learns how each upstream performs by kind
of request and transport, orders upstreams for every request by the strategy
you choose, and fails over in-flight. You keep deep control over your
infrastructure, node failures stay away from your users, and your app keeps
speaking standard JSON-RPC.

An agent can create a working endpoint with one HTTP request and no signup.
Start with the [quickstart](/cloud/quickstart), or read on to decide whether
Lasso fits your app.

```bash
curl -sS https://lasso.sh/api/v1/keys \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"name":"prod"}'
```

## How a request flows

```text
your app ──▶ https://lasso.sh/rpc/k/<key>/fastest/base
               │
               ├─ finds providers in the profile that serve the chain and method
               ├─ orders them by your strategy
               ├─ sends the request, failing over on errors that are safe to retry
               └─ returns standard JSON-RPC, plus routing evidence on request
```

It's drop-in: viem, ethers, Foundry, Hardhat and indexers keep speaking
JSON-RPC to a new URL. You choose three things in that URL, and nothing else in
your app changes:

- **Your routing strategy**, per call site: `load-balanced`,
  `latency-weighted`, `fastest` or `priority`.
- **Your provider pool**: Lasso's `premium` pool, its `public` pool, or a
  Custom profile over your own nodes and provider accounts. One key reaches
  all of them; `/profile/<name>` picks one.
- **Your chain**: any EVM chain Lasso serves, or any EVM chain your own
  providers serve.

## When Lasso fits

| Your situation | What Lasso gives you |
|---|---|
| A provider's outages or rate limits show up as errors in your app | Failover across a pool of providers behind one URL |
| Latency matters: trading, wallets, bots | `fastest` and `latency-weighted` routing, measured from the region serving you |
| Backends and indexers exhaust one provider's quota | Load spread across providers with `load-balanced` |
| You already pay for provider accounts or run your own nodes | A Custom profile routes over them, reachable with the key you already have |
| Subscriptions must survive a provider outage | WebSocket failover that backfills the gap |
| An agent is setting up your infrastructure | One-request provisioning, pay-as-you-go in USDC, and a handoff to your team |

## What stays with your app

- **Transactions.** Signing, nonces, replacement, receipts and finality stay
  with your app. Lasso sends a signed transaction to one provider and doesn't
  retry it after dispatch. If the response is lost, look up the transaction
  hash before resending.
- **HTTP filters.** Filter IDs belong to one provider, so use `eth_getLogs` or
  subscriptions instead.
- **Provider limits.** Historical depth and `eth_getLogs` range limits follow
  the providers in your pool.

## How you pay

Requests on Lasso's premium providers have a price in USD, set by the method
and your strategy. Your account pays from its included usage first, then from
its balance. If your account has paid before and the balance runs out, your
app keeps serving on public providers for 30 days while you top up. Accounts
with a person get a monthly allowance on public providers at no charge. See
[serving access and pricing](/cloud/serving-access-and-pricing) and
[balance and payment fallback](/cloud/balance-and-fallback).

## Two ways in, one account

| | Agents, through the API | People, through the dashboard |
|---|---|---|
| Start | `POST /api/v1/keys`, no signup | Sign in at [lasso.sh](https://lasso.sh) and create an endpoint |
| Fund | Pay a 402 challenge with a USDC wallet | Add balance, or subscribe to Pro or Custom |
| Manage | A management token | The dashboard |

Both work on the same account. A person can claim what an agent built without
changing a single URL. See [accounts, people and agents](/cloud/accounts-and-agents).

## Limits

- EVM chains only. The live chain list is in
  [chains and prices](/cloud/reference/chains-and-prices) and
  [`agent.json`](https://lasso.sh/agent.json).
- Routing evidence shows which provider answered, not that the answer is
  correct.
- Latency is measured per kind of request (a family of related methods), per
  region.
